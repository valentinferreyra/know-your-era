# Historical content review

The bank covers main PPV broadcasts from WrestleMania XIV through Vengeance 2004. The event catalog contains 88 eligible editions, including the UK-only broadcasts. The official WWE archive catalog establishes event inclusion; the historical supercard table supplies date and final-match references. Main-event facts refer to the closing broadcast match, not the most promoted match.

## Evidence requirements

Each reviewed question must reference reviewed facts and inspected supporting sources. Read full result entries, not search snippets. Preserve exact event dates and distinguish the champion entering a match from the eventual winner. Use championship histories or independent corroboration when an archived result has contradictory wording. Record story segments from a contemporary report, official clip, or the broadcast. Verify within-show sequence explicitly.

Reviewed is an editorial assertion, not a claim that the automated validator can establish historical truth. Tests check references and structure; the answer still needs direct comparison with the inspected evidence.

## Known archive discrepancies

- WWE's Vengeance 2004 results page lists Kane defeating Matt Hardy. The owner observed Matt winning, which requires corroboration from the event/broadcast or another reliable record before a winner question is authored. Do not use the erroneous result as the sole evidence.
- WWE's No Mercy 2003 summary describes Tajiri winning the Cruiserweight title. Verify the entering champion separately before using this wording.
- WWE's Backlash 2000 summary calls Dean Malenko's title the Cruiserweight Championship. Verify the historical Light Heavyweight title identity before authoring a title question.
- WWE's Survivor Series 1999 landing-page summary uses the wrong event year in its prose. Prefer the actual results article and independently verified event date.

## Question review checklist

1. The stated event and main-broadcast fact are within scope.
2. The exact answer follows from the cited entry, with all names and titles checked.
3. Three distractors genuinely do not satisfy a single-choice prompt.
4. An odd-one-out group has exactly one exception.
5. Matching forms a bijection with no multi-person match silently represented as a singles bout.
6. Chronology statements refer to the recorded incident, not a later admission, appearance, or reveal. Same-night order is explicit and verified.
7. Difficulty reflects recognizable knowledge and temporal proximity, not obscure wording or trivia.
8. Independent families are not duplicate reformulations. Shared facts and answer-revealing relationships receive conflict groups.
9. Optional logos may represent any edition, but visible contradictory year or edition markings cannot mislead. Prompt photos do not reveal the answer.
10. Questions, explanations, and visible answer labels use English and WWE wording; historical source terminology stays internal.
